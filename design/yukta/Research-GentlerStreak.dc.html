Gentler Streak gets the values right and the claims wrong

The closest app to Yukta's values: on-device, kind, built on your own ranges. Reviewed as Yukta's target user would use it: losing weight, on iPhone, often without an Apple Watch. Screens are from its Spanish App Store listing, translated.

  1. 6 · Burned calories as junk-food equivalentsHighA wrist estimate (often 20–30% or more off) turned into burger and fries icons: "earn your food" framing, the opposite of gentle for anyone managing weight.
  2. 7 · A runner's dashboard inside a gentle appMediumVO₂ max, zones, splits, cadence and power on one screen. Two audiences, one app, and the casual one pays for the complexity.
8False precision about the bodyHigh

"You're perfectly meeting your body's needs and capacities" and "You're being productive". The first claims accuracy no sensor has; the second turns activity into a moral score.

9Verdicts without a "because"Medium

"You can take it easy" or "A great day to push yourself" never names the inputs. The developer added a "What's Activity Path" button because people didn't get it.

10Built around Apple WatchMedium

The developer says sparse heart-rate data makes the path less accurate, and manual workout logging is Premium. A Fitbit Air user on iPhone gets the weakest version for the same price. Nothing on food or weight at all.

Where it sets the bar

"100% privacy, on the device", no accounts, no data to AI models or ad platforms (per its docs). Kind tone, personal ranges instead of universal targets, widgets and watch complications for glanceable answers. Apple's App of the Year and Apple Design Award, per its listing. This is the app Yukta's values most resemble, so it is the one to beat.

Where Yukta differs

Free with no Premium; energy balance at the core, which Gentler Streak doesn't touch; any wearable through Apple Health, not Watch-first; every claim sourced instead of encouraging; calm and native instead of a mascot.
