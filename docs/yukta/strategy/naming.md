# Yukta: the name and what it means

**Decided 13 November 2026:** the first open-nomadapps app is called **Yukta** (युक्त).
Pending checks: App Store name availability, trademarks (India, US, EU) and domains. Earlier
working names: "readiness" (to 12 November), "balance" (12–13 November).

**Pronunciation:** *YOOK-tuh* (IAST *yukta*; the *u* as in "put", a short final *a*).

## In one line

*Yukta* is Sanskrit for **balanced, moderate, regulated; fitting; joined**. In Bhagavad Gītā 6.17
it names exactly the formula this app is built on: moderation in eating, activity, effort and
sleep, as the ground for a calm, focused mind.

## The word

- **Root:** the verb √*yuj*, "to yoke, to join". *Yukta* is its past participle: "yoked, joined",
  and so "fitting, appropriate, held in measure".
- **Same root as *yoga***: *yoga* is the joining; *yukta* is the one who is joined, steady and in
  balance. The name says the app is about the practice, not a product.

## Bhagavad Gītā 6.17

From chapter 6, *Dhyāna-yoga*, "the yoga of meditation", where Krishna describes the conditions
that make a steady, meditative mind possible.

**Devanāgarī**

> युक्ताहारविहारस्य युक्तचेष्टस्य कर्मसु ।
> युक्तस्वप्नावबोधस्य योगो भवति दुःखहा ॥ ६.१७ ॥

**Transliteration (IAST)**

> yuktāhāra-vihārasya yukta-ceṣṭasya karmasu
> yukta-svapnāvabodhasya yogo bhavati duḥkha-hā

**Word by word**

| Sanskrit | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| *yukta* | balanced, moderate, regulated (used three times in the verse) |
| *āhāra* | food, what is taken in |
| *vihāra* | recreation, moving about, leisure |
| *yukta-ceṣṭasya* | of one whose efforts (*ceṣṭā*) are measured |
| *karmasu* | in actions, in work |
| *svapna* | sleep |
| *avabodha* | waking, being awake |
| *yogaḥ bhavati* | yoga becomes |
| *duḥkha-hā* | the destroyer of suffering (*duḥkha* "sorrow" + *-hā* "that which removes") |

**Translation**

> For one who is moderate in eating and recreation, measured in effort at work, and regulated in
> sleep and waking, yoga becomes the remover of sorrow.

Translations differ in wording, not in substance: "regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping,
recreation and work" (Prabhupāda); "temperate in eating and recreation, balanced in work, and
regulated in sleep" (Mukundananda); Śaṅkara's commentary renders the last part as "whose sleep
and wakefulness are temperate".

### Its companion verse, 6.16

The verse just before it says what *yukta* rules out: both extremes.

> नात्यश्नतस्तु योगोऽस्ति न चैकान्तमनश्नतः ।
> न चातिस्वप्नशीलस्य जाग्रतो नैव चार्जुन ॥ ६.१६ ॥
>
> nātyaśnatas tu yogo 'sti na caikāntam anaśnataḥ
> na cāti-svapna-śīlasya jāgrato naiva cārjuna

> Yoga is not for one who eats too much, nor for one who does not eat at all; not for one who
> sleeps too much, nor for one who stays awake too long, O Arjuna.

Commentators read the pair as moderation, not self-denial: overeating and fasting both stand in the
way, and so do too much and too little sleep.

## How the verse maps onto the app

| In the verse | In Yukta |
|--------------|----------|
| *yukta-āhāra*: moderate eating | **Energy:** intake measured against your real expenditure; protein |
| *vihāra*: recreation, moving about | **Movement:** steps against your own normal |
| *yukta-ceṣṭā karmasu*: measured effort | **Strength:** two sessions a week, never "more is better" |
| *yukta-svapnāvabodha*: regulated sleep and waking | **Sleep:** duration and timing consistency |
| Chapter 6 is *Dhyāna-yoga*, the yoga of meditation | **Mindfulness and recovery:** the v1.1 recovery pillar (HRV, resting heart rate); mindful minutes from Apple Health are a candidate for later |
| 6.16: neither too much nor too little | **Guardrails:** the calorie floor, the loss-rate cap, no deficits for minors or low BMI |
| *duḥkha-hā*: removes sorrow | **Not claimed.** The app makes no promise beyond its evidence (manifesto rule 10) |

## The same idea in Ayurveda

| Text | What it says |
|------|--------------|
| **Charaka Saṃhitā, Sūtrasthāna 11.35**: *trayopastambha*, the three sub-pillars of life | *Āhāra* (food), *nidrā* (sleep) and *brahmacarya* (regulated conduct, self-restraint), kept in proper measure through *yukti*, reasoned planning (from the same root again) |
| **Charaka Saṃhitā, Sūtrasthāna 7.31–35**: *vyāyāma*, exercise | Suitable movement that brings stability and strength; it gives lightness and endurance; it has signs to stop, and in excess it harms |

So two of the oldest Indian sources on how to live (a scripture and a medical text) land where
modern evidence does: eat, move and sleep in measure. That convergence is the story of the name.

## Why not Amruta

Shirish's first idea was *amṛta* (अमृत, "Amruta"). It is a beautiful word, but:

- **It means "deathless", the nectar of immortality.** In the Rigveda it is a name for *soma*, the
  drink that makes the gods immortal; in the *Samudra Manthana* the gods and demons churn the ocean
  to win it. It names an outcome, immortality, not a practice. That is the kind of promise the app
  refuses to make (manifesto rule 10).
- **It is a crowded name.** Amrutam Ayurveda is already in Health & Fitness on the App Store and
  reads Apple Health; Amrita hospitals and Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham are large brands in India; and
  in Ayurveda the herb Guḍūcī is also called *Amṛtā* (from memory), which suggests herbal remedies.

## Using the verse respectfully

- Quote it with its reference, in Devanāgarī and transliteration, and give its meaning plainly.
- No religious imagery, deities or devotional framing in the app or the listing. The app is for
  everyone and makes no spiritual claims.
- Acknowledge that traditions read the verse differently (Śaṅkara, Abhinavagupta, Vaiṣṇava and
  Vedānta commentators); we cite the shared plain meaning, moderation.
- Before quoting a Charaka verse in the app, check it against a critical edition (for example
  Sharma and Dash).
